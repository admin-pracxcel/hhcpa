Q28. A RED OUTCOME PROMISES CONTACT, BUT NO CONTACT DETAILS HAVE BEEN COLLECTED.
     *** This is a build hole, not a preference, and it is a REGRESSION. ***
     Note first: the current quiz collects contact details from EVERYONE, as the last
     step before submission, after the branches and before triage is computed. Red
     patients therefore reach the team today. The v2.3 architecture removes that by
     making the only contact capture part of the Step 6 intake, green and amber only.
     The v2.3 s1 architecture is:
        Step 0-2  gates          (no contact details)
        Step 3    service select (no contact details)
        Step 4    eligibility    (her question sets - no contact details)
        Step 5    triage computed
        Step 6    intake form    - explicitly "green and amber only"
        Step 7    booking, or red exit
     Her red exit message is: "Based on your responses, your situation requires further
     review before booking. A member of our team will contact you."
     Nothing in Steps 0 to 5 captures a name, email or phone. On a red outcome the site
     promises a callback it has no way to make, and v2.3 s9 assigns someone to own that
     queue - a queue that would be empty.
     Deliberately staging the intake form after triage is the right call (v2.3 s1 is
     correct that a red patient should not have handed over a residential address
     first). The fix is a minimal capture on the red exit only: name, and one of email
     or phone, with consent to be contacted. Confirm that is wanted, and confirm the
     exact consent wording.

Q29. THE PEPTIDE DECISION REVERSES v2.1, AND WILL FAIL THE BUILD AS IT STANDS.
     v2.1 s4.7.1 said FRM-007's two peptide lines "must not ship as written" and gave

     contains a prohibited term."

         build team has not seen that instruction. Please confirm who gave it and put it
         on the record, exactly as v2.3 s7 itself asks.
     (b) The automated guard test added in the last compliance pass fails the build the

         That is a deliberate, auditable hole in the compliance net; confirm it is

SECTION F — BUILD AND IMPLEMENTATION QUESTIONS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------

These are not compliance or copy questions. They are decisions the build has to make
that the spec does not cover, where guessing wrong is expensive to undo. Grouped by
area. Where the build team has a recommendation it is stated, so several of these can
be answered with a yes.

--- The quiz engine ---

Q33. ONE /quiz, OR EIGHT PER-SERVICE ENTRY POINTS?
     v2.3 s1 shows a single flow with service selection at Step 3. But v2.1 s4.1.4 says
     the homepage box goes to the service page and "the service page prompts the
     pre-screening questions before booking", which implies each service page deep-links
     into the quiz with Step 3 already answered.
     Build as one flow at /quiz that accepts a pre-selected service (for example
     /quiz?service=mens-health), so the eight service pages can link straight in and
     skip Step 3? That is the build team's recommendation - one engine, eight doors.
     If instead you want eight separate quiz URLs, say so now; it changes the routing,
     the analytics and the sitemap.

Q34. CAN A PATIENT GO BACK AND CHANGE A TRIAGE-DETERMINING ANSWER?
     The current quiz has a real Back button with a step history stack, so a patient who
     hits an exit can reverse out and change the answer that caused it. That is already
     on record as a compliance flag: the existing "Unable to Proceed" exits name the
     answer that disqualified the patient, so a second run-through passes, and AHPRA
     warns specifically about questionnaires that coach patients to say the right thing.
     With triage now formalised into green/amber/red, this matters more, not less.
     Options: (a) keep Back fully free; (b) keep Back but lock answers once triage is
     computed at Step 5; (c) keep Back but stop exit screens from naming the
     disqualifying answer. The build team recommends (b) and (c) together.

Q35. SHOULD PARTIAL PROGRESS SURVIVE A REFRESH?
     The flow becomes 7 steps plus a ~25-field intake form, 5 to 7 minutes. Nothing is
     persisted today; a refresh loses everything. Persisting means writing partial
     health information, and after Step 6 a residential address, into browser storage.
     Build team recommendation: persist the pre-triage steps only (gates, service, and
     screening answers) in sessionStorage so a refresh is not fatal, and never persist
     the Step 6 intake fields or the signature. Confirm, or say no persistence at all.

Q36. WHAT IS THE SUBMISSION PAYLOAD CONTRACT FOR n8n?
     Today one webhook receives a payload with clinical answers segregated under a
     dedicated `clinical` key, so n8n can route them to the clinical destination and
     strip them before anything else sees them. The new flow adds: gate answers, the
     Step 4 screening set, the computed triage outcome, a ~25-field intake form
     including residential address and emergency contact, plus signature, timestamp and
     form ID/version.
     Questions: does the `clinical` segregation still apply, and should the intake PII
     go under its own separate key rather than being merged into `contact`? Is it one
     webhook or one per stage (a light triage submission, then a heavier intake
     submission)? The build team recommends keeping `clinical` segregation, adding an
     `intake` key for the FRM fields with the signature block inside it, and keeping
     one webhook.

Q37. ONE n8n OUTCOME FLOW, OR ONE PER SERVICE?
     Eight services times three outcomes is 24 combinations. This decides what the site
     emits: a single `{service, outcome}` pair for n8n to branch on, or a resolved
     routing key per combination.
     Build team recommendation: emit `{service, outcome}` and let n8n branch, so adding
     a ninth service never needs a site deploy. Confirm.

--- File upload ---

Q38. WHERE DOES THE DISCHARGE LETTER FILE ACTUALLY GO?
     v2.3 s8 requires a file upload and correctly lists virus scanning, size and type
     limits, encrypted storage and privacy coverage. None of that infrastructure exists.
     There is no backend and no object storage; the site posts to n8n webhooks.
     Which of these: (a) post the file to n8n as base64 and let the workflow handle
     scanning and storage; (b) stand up object storage (S3, R2 or similar) with a
     signed-upload flow; (c) use a third-party form/upload service; (d) ship the page
     without the upload for now, with the "help me obtain one" free-text field only,
     and add upload when a destination exists?
     This is the largest new piece of infrastructure in the whole brief and it needs a
     decision before the page is built. Note (d) still delivers the page and the
     discount.

--- Page architecture ---

Q39. DO THE THREE NEW HUBS USE THE EXISTING SERVICE PAGE TEMPLATE?
     Every service page renders through one data-driven template with a fixed module
     vocabulary (hero, trust bar, intro, split, tiles, statement, checklist, steps,
     pricing cue, related cards, inline CTA, FAQ, closing band). Building the new hubs
     the same way means they inherit the schema, breadcrumbs, gating and compliance
     guards automatically.
     Confirm the new hubs use it, rather than bespoke layouts. Recommendation: yes.

Q40. ARE THE THREE NEW HUBS GATED AT LAUNCH?
     The route registry has a `gated` flag built for exactly this: one flag hides a page
     from nav, footer, related cards, focus grid, sitemap and robots at once. It was
     used for the previous regulated pages until she signed off in writing, and s7.4
     requires her express written approval before any regulated page publishes.
     Do the three new hubs ship gated until she approves them, or ungated on the
     staging build so she can review them in place? Recommendation: build ungated on
     staging (which is noindex anyway) and gate at production until sign-off.

Q41. HEALTH OPTIMISATION'S SEVEN SUB-AREAS - SECTIONS NOW, PAGES LATER?
     v2.1 s3.1 says "rendered as on-page sections at launch". "At launch" implies they
     become child pages later.
     Should the URL structure be reserved now (/health-optimisation/metabolic-health/
     and so on), or built purely as on-page anchors with no child routes? Reserving
     later costs a round of redirects. Recommendation: on-page anchors only for now,
     since seven thin child pages would compete with the hub.

Q42. MENTAL HEALTH SUPPORT - WHICH BREADCRUMB?
     It is promoted to a top-level nav group but keeps /online-doctor/mental-health.
     Its breadcrumb and BreadcrumbList schema currently read Home > Online Doctor >
     Mental Health Support, matching the URL.
     Does the breadcrumb follow the new nav position (Home > Mental Health Support) or
     stay with the URL? Recommendation: stay with the URL, because Google cross-checks
     BreadcrumbList against the path and a mismatch is a structured-data warning.

Q43. LAYOUT OF EIGHT BOXES AND EIGHT MEGA-MENU SILOS.
     (a) The homepage and /services grid goes from five boxes to eight. The grid centres
     a short final row, so eight lands as 3 + 3 + 2. Acceptable, or do you want 4 + 4?
     (b) The mega-menu goes from four silos to eight. It is a four-column panel, so
     eight silos means two rows. Three of the new silos have no child pages (Health
     Optimisation, Continuity, Holistic Care) and neither does Mental Health Support.
     The existing stacking mechanism lets a childless silo sit beneath another one in
     the same column while keeping its own heading, which avoids four headings with
     nothing under them. Confirm that is the shape you want, and confirm the pairing:
     recommendation is Weight Management + Health Optimisation, Men's Health, Women's
     Health, Online Doctor + Mental Health Support, with Continuity and Holistic Care
     stacked under Men's and Women's respectively.
